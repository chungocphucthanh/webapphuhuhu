# 2.5 Soft constraints

> Owner: Võ Nguyễn Vĩ Nguyên. Code: `m2_ilp/soft.py`. Verification: `m2_ilp/test_soft.py`.

Three desiderata are added to the stage-2 objective function as weighted penalties
$\lambda_{LOC} = 0.84$, $\lambda_{FAT} = 1.9$, $\lambda_{TRV} = 1.97$ (D3).

## The three penalties

**LOC – campus preference.** The preference $pref(i) \in \{CS1, CS2, BAL\}$ is simulated data generated from the seed
(section 2.6). The cost is
$$c_{ij} = \begin{cases}1 & pref(i)\in\{CS1,CS2\}\ \text{and}\ pref(i) \ne campus(j)\\ 0 & \text{otherwise (including BAL)}\end{cases},\qquad LOC = \sum_{(i,j)\in A} c_{ij}\,x_{ij}.$$
No new variables are needed.

**FAT – fatigue.** For each person $i$ and day $d$:
$$s_{id} \ge \sum_{j \in J(d),\,(i,j)\in A} x_{ij} - K \quad(\texttt{FAT}_{id}),\qquad s_{id}\ge 0,\qquad FAT = \sum s_{id}.$$

**TRV – travel between campuses within a day** (forum d=33630 treats this as a soft cost):
$$z_{i,d,c(j)} \ge x_{ij}\ \ (\texttt{TRVZ}_{ij}),\qquad v_{id} \ge z_{id1} + z_{id2} - 1\ \ (\texttt{TRV}_{id}),\qquad TRV = \sum v_{id}.$$

## Why $s$, $z$, $v$ can remain continuous and still be correct

The problem is a minimization and the weights are positive, so at the optimum each auxiliary variable equals exactly its lower bound:
$s_{id} = \max(0, \text{daily load} - K)$ and $v_{id} = \max(0, z_{id1}+z_{id2}-1)$. When $x$ is integral,
$x_{ij}=1$ pushes $z_{i,d,c(j)}$ up to 1; if $i$ does not work at campus $c$, then $z_{idc}$ can be 0.
Hence $v_{id}=1$ exactly when $i$ works at both campuses on day $d$. The test
`test_evaluate_soft_equals_the_model_terms_on_a_real_slice` confirms that the values of the three expressions in the
model equal the values computed in plain Python (`evaluate_soft`) at the solver's solution, and
`fixtures.forced_soft_instance` forces all three penalties to be positive (LOC = 2, FAT = 1, TRV = 1).

## Create variables only when the penalty can be positive

- $s_{id}$ only when $\lvert A_i \cap J(d)\rvert > K$: otherwise the daily load cannot exceed $K$.
- $z_{idc}, v_{id}$ only when $i$ has available sessions at **both** campuses on day $d$.

## Choosing the threshold $K$ from data (TV5-2, "fit, don't fabricate")

Distribution of the number of sessions per person on each working day of the baseline (472 person–day pairs):

| Sessions/day | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of person–day pairs | 246 | 162 | 57 | 7 |
| Cumulative share $\le$ | 52.1% | 86.4% | 98.5% | 100% |

Rule: $K$ is the smallest value that at least 95% of the baseline's person–day pairs do not exceed
(`soft.fit_fatigue_k`). Result: $K = 3$: only the 7 cases of 4 sessions/day in the original schedule are considered
fatiguing. $K$ is a **penalty** threshold, not a hard limit. The effect of $K-1, K, K+1$ is in section 2.8.

## Results

| Term | Baseline | M2 |
|---|---|---|
| LOC (sessions against preference) | 162 | 28 |
| FAT (sessions exceeding $K = 3$) | 7 | 0 |
| TRV (days working at both campuses) | 0 | 0 |

M2 reduces LOC from 162 to 28 and eliminates all 7 overloaded days, while still keeping the optimal max load of 11.

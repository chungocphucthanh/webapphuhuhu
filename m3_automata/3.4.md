# 3.4 Bounded balance and exact balance

The bounded rule R4 is built by `r4` in [rules.py](rules.py) and exported to [extra/dfa_R4.json](extra/dfa_R4.json). The proof that exact balance is not regular is in [pumping.md](pumping.md).

## Definitions

Let $\#_ M(w)$ and $\#_ A(w)$ count the morning and afternoon symbols of $w\in\Sigma^{\ast}$, and $d(w)=\#_ M(w)-\#_ A(w)$. For $c\in\mathbb{N}$:

$$
L_ c=\lbrace w: |d(u)|\leq c\ \text{for every prefix } u \text{ of } w\rbrace,
\qquad
L_ {=}=\lbrace w: \#_ M(w)=\#_ A(w)\rbrace.
$$

## R4 is regular

The prefix $u$ only matters through $d(u)$ while $|d(u)|\leq c$, and an excess cannot be undone because every prefix is checked. Take $Q=\lbrace -c,\ldots,c\rbrace\cup\lbrace\mathrm{dead}\rbrace$, start $0$, and $F=\lbrace -c,\ldots,c\rbrace$:

$$
\delta(d,M)=\begin{cases}d+1,& d<c\cr \mathrm{dead},& d=c\end{cases}
\qquad
\delta(d,A)=\begin{cases}d-1,& d>-c\cr \mathrm{dead},& d=-c\end{cases}
\qquad
\delta(d,N)=\delta(d,\texttt{-})=d,
$$

and `dead` loops on every symbol. The invariant is that a valid prefix $u$ ends in state $d(u)$ and an invalid one in `dead`. It holds for the empty word, each step preserves it, and `dead` is never left. So $L(\mathcal{A})=L_ c$, with $2c+1$ live states and one trap, $2c+2$ in total: 8 for $c=3$. No two live states are equivalent, since for $d<d'$ the word $M^{c-d'+1}$ sends $d'$ to `dead` and keeps $d$ alive, so 8 is also the minimal size.

## Exact balance is not regular

If $L_ {=}$ were regular, then $L_ {=}\cap M^{\ast}A^{\ast}=\lbrace M^nA^n: n\geq 0\rbrace$ would be regular, because regular languages are closed under intersection. The pumping lemma refutes this; the proof is in [pumping.md](pumping.md).

## Why the ILP carries the exact count

A DFA that checks $d(w)=0$ over a horizon of $T$ days needs states $-T,\ldots,T$, so its size grows with $T$, and the exact rule cannot be one fixed automaton for every horizon. In the ILP of [3.3](3.3.md) the same rule is one linear equation per staff member, $\sum_ t z_ {i,t,M}-\sum_ t z_ {i,t,A}=0$, with no new states. A bounded rule such as R4 fits an automaton because its number of states depends on $c$ only.

## Small example

For $c=3$ and $w=\texttt{MMMMAA}$ the run is

$$0\xrightarrow{M}1\xrightarrow{M}2\xrightarrow{M}3\xrightarrow{M}\mathrm{dead}\xrightarrow{A}\mathrm{dead}\xrightarrow{A}\mathrm{dead}.$$

The final difference is $d(w)=2\leq 3$, yet the prefix `MMMM` has $d=4$, so $w$ is rejected under the prefix reading.

On the 73 strings of [encode_strings.py](encode_strings.py), R4 with $c=3$ rejects 20 strings under the prefix reading and 19 if only the end of the term is checked. The end-of-term values of $|d|$ over the 73 staff are

$$0{:}8,\ 1{:}11,\ 2{:}18,\ 3{:}17,\ 4{:}8,\ 5{:}8,\ 6{:}2,\ 7{:}1.$$

The extra rejected string is CB020: it reaches $d=4$ on day 17 and ends at $d=1$.

## Functions in the code

- `r4(c=3)`: the complete DFA with $2c+2$ states, alphabet $\lbrace M,A,N,\texttt{-}\rbrace$, start `0` and trap `dead`.
- `r4_counts(strings, c=3)`: numbers of strings rejected by the prefix reading and by the end-of-term reading.
- `main()`: writes the DFA JSON of R1 to R3 next to `rules.py` and R4 to `extra/dfa_R4.json`.

## Limits

The encoder keeps one symbol per day with priority $N>A>M$, so a morning and afternoon day counts as $A$: 168 of the 169 two-class days are of this kind, and the balance is shifted towards $A$. Adding R4 to the product raises the minimal DFA from 15 to 99 states (see [3.2](3.2.md)), which is why the ILP of 3.3 links R1, R2 and R3 only and R4 is handled as a DFA and in the proof.
